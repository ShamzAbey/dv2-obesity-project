Australian public health data story

Australian Fast Food & Obesity

Exploring how diet, activity, demographics, and geography shape obesity patterns across Australia.

Introduction

Why average Australians should care

Obesity is a major public health issue because it is linked to long-term chronic disease, wellbeing, and pressure on health services. The story is not about blaming individuals. It is about showing how geography, sugary drinks, food environments, physical inactivity, age, and gender combine to create different health-risk patterns across Australia.

What

State polygons, locations, age groups, gender groups, beverage trends, physical activity rates, and BMI categories.

Why

Compare places, identify high-risk groups, inspect patterns over time, and explore relationships between lifestyle factors.

How

Maps, proportional symbols, brushing, dropdowns, legend filtering, small multiples, Sankey flow, radar profile, and dashboard coordination.

Question

Where are obesity rates highest, and which lifestyle indicators appear alongside those patterns?

Audience

Designed for average Australians who need a quick but honest public-health explanation, not a technical report.

Data rule

Only public Australian government data is used. Where direct fast-food data is unavailable, the page labels the real substitute measure clearly.

Section 4 | Geographic pattern

Obesity rates are not evenly distributed

The first view uses a choropleth map to encode adult obesity by state and territory. This is the clearest first step because the task is geographic comparison.

Map idiom: choropleth Task: compare regions Interaction: filter, zoom, tooltip
Graph 1

Choropleth map: adult obesity by state

How to read it. Darker states have a higher percentage of adults classified as obese in the ABS 2022 National Health Survey.

Why this chart first. It establishes the geographic pattern before the story moves into behaviour, time, and demographics.

Source: ABS National Health Survey: State and territory findings, 2022. Use the state filter, zoom and pan sliders, and hover tooltips to inspect each state.

Section 5 | Fast food geography

Unhealthy consumption geography

Direct state-level fast-food consumption was not available in the public tables used here. To avoid fake data, this map uses the real ABS state-level daily sugar-sweetened drink measure as the available unhealthy food/drink indicator.

Map idiom: proportional symbol Measure: sugar-sweetened drinks Encoding: size plus colour
Graph 2

Proportional symbol map: sugar-sweetened drinks

How to read it. Circle size represents daily sugar-sweetened drink consumption, while colour keeps adult obesity visible on the same map.

Data honesty. This is not labelled as fast-food frequency because that state-level field was not available in the selected public sources.

Source: ABS National Health Survey: State and territory findings, 2017-18; ABS NHS 2022 for obesity. Circle size shows daily sugar-sweetened drink consumption; colour shows obesity.

Weight status has shifted over survey years

The historical state series available from ABS reports adults overweight or obese. The time-slider map keeps the temporal map idiom while clearly naming the measure.

Map idiom: time-slider choropleth Task: compare years Control: survey step slider
Graph 3

Time-slider map: adults overweight or obese

How to read it. Move through ABS survey years to see whether the spatial pattern is stable or changing.

Section 7 | Unhealthy drinks vs obesity

Consumption and obesity can be compared directly

A bubble plot makes the relationship between daily sugar-sweetened drink consumption and obesity visible, while population size keeps state scale in the story.

Idiom: bubble scatterplot Task: inspect relationship Encoding: x, y, size, colour
Graph 4

Bubble plot: sugar-sweetened drinks vs obesity

How to read it. States further right have higher sugary-drink consumption; states higher up have higher obesity.

Important caution. The trend line is an association only. It does not prove that one variable causes the other.

Use the sex dropdown, state dropdown, legend filter, and tooltips. Bubble size represents population.

Idiom: correlation matrix Task: compare variables Interpretation: exploratory

Correlation matrix

How to read it. Positive values move together; negative values move in opposite directions.

Caution. With only eight states and territories, use this to generate questions rather than final claims.
