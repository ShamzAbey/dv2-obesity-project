Australian public health data story

Australian Fast Food & Obesity

Exploring relationships between obesity, food consumption, demographics, and physical activity across Australia.

Why it matters

Obesity is shaped by everyday environments

Obesity is increasing across Australia and is influenced by diet, demographics, physical activity, and geographic differences. This project uses Australian government data to help everyday Australians compare state patterns, age and gender differences, and food-consumption signals without needing to read dense statistical tables.

Geography

Where obesity is highest

Start with the map, then switch indicators and use the zoom/pan controls to inspect how obesity and weight status vary across states and territories.

Graph 1

Interactive choropleth map

Uses local Australian TopoJSON with ABS National Health Survey values joined from CSV. The dropdown changes the mapped health indicator.

Food environment

Food and beverage patterns create context

Direct state-level fast-food tables are limited in the available public data, so the bubble and profile views use transparent food-context proxy indices derived from real ABS beverage, activity, and health measures. The labels make that clear instead of pretending they are raw fast-food counts.

Graph 2

Bubble plot: food-context proxy and obesity

Bubble size represents state population. Use the legend and risk-band dropdown to filter the comparison.

Graph 3

Overview + detail time series

Brush the lower overview chart and click legend categories to focus the detailed ABS beverage trend above.

People

Age and gender change the story

Age and gender comparisons reveal patterns that disappear in a single national average. Older age groups generally show higher obesity and overweight/obesity rates.

Graph 4

Small multiples: age and gender

Switch between obesity and overweight/obesity to compare how men and women differ across age groups.

Graph 5

Radar chart: selected state profiles

Radar-style lines compare obesity, weight status, inactivity, and food-context proxy indicators for selected states.

Lifestyle profile

Activity sits beside food and weight status

Physical activity is only one factor, but it helps explain how different places sit within a broader lifestyle-risk profile.

Graph 6

Parallel coordinates profile

Use the legend to highlight a state and compare obesity, activity, inactivity, and food-context proxy indicators.

Graph 7

Sankey diagram: obesity to activity category

Flow thickness shows how many states fall into each obesity-to-activity category pathway.

Behaviour matrix

Diet and obesity by age

Diet recommendation achievement is low for vegetables across all adult age groups, while obesity and overweight/obesity rise in older groups.

Graph 8

Heatmap: age group health and diet behaviour

Graph 9
