Australian Fast Food & Obesity

Exploring the relationship between obesity, food consumption, and physical activity across Australia.

Introduction

Obesity is a growing public health issue in Australia, with long-term impacts on chronic disease, wellbeing, and health services. Lifestyle factors such as food consumption, physical activity, and demographic differences all contribute to obesity patterns. This visualisation explores these patterns across states, genders, and age groups using Australian government data from the ABS and AIHW.

Processed, convenience, and takeaway-style food consumption patterns have changed over time. These trends may relate to rising obesity rates because food environments influence the everyday choices available to Australians.

Physical Activity and Obesity

Lower physical activity may relate to higher obesity rates, although the relationship is not the only factor affecting public health outcomes. A scatterplot helps compare two variables at once, showing how each state and territory sits against the broader pattern.

Advanced scatterplot: physical activity vs obesity

Bubble size shows population, with a regression trend line, mean reference lines, labels for key states, and detailed tooltips. Source: ABS National Health Survey: State and territory findings, 2022, and ABS population at 30 June 2022.

Lifestyle risk index

The index combines adult obesity with the share not meeting activity guidelines, then compares states against risk bands and the mean.

Bin map: activity-obesity clusters

The binned map groups states by physical activity and obesity ranges, with points showing the original state values.

Sankey diagram: obesity category to activity category

Sankey-style links group states by obesity category and physical activity category; link thickness shows the number of states/territories.

Obesity Across Gender and Age Groups

Obesity affects demographic groups differently. Comparing age and gender reveals important patterns that are hidden in national averages, especially where older age groups experience higher obesity rates.

Gender difference by age group

Source: ABS National Health Survey 2022 Table 8.3 measured BMI proportions. Dumbbell links show the gap between men and women.

Node-link diagram: obesity by gender and age

The node-link diagram connects adults to gender and age groups; age-node colour shows obesity rate and node size shows overweight/obese percentage.

Dendrogram: obesity by gender and age

The dendrogram presents the same age-gender hierarchy as a tree, with node colour and size encoding health rates.

Histogram: age-gender obesity distribution

The histogram bins obesity rates across age and gender groups and marks the overall mean.

Key Findings

Obesity is influenced by multiple lifestyle factors, including food consumption, physical activity, geography, age, and gender. The visualisations show that geographic and demographic patterns are visible in Australian public health data, and that data visualisation can communicate complex health trends in a clear and accessible way.
