Australian public health data story

Australian Fast Food & Obesity

Exploring how diet, activity, demographics, and geography shape obesity patterns across Australia.

Introduction

Why average Australians should care

Obesity is a major public health issue because it is linked to long-term chronic disease, wellbeing, and pressure on health services. The story is not about blaming individuals. It is about showing how geography, sugary drinks, food environments, physical inactivity, age, and gender combine to create different health-risk patterns across Australia.

What

State polygons, locations, age groups, gender groups, beverage trends, physical activity rates, and BMI categories.

Why

Compare places, identify high-risk groups, inspect patterns over time, and explore relationships between lifestyle factors.

How

Maps, proportional symbols, brushing, dropdowns, legend filtering, small multiples, Sankey flow, radar profile, and dashboard coordination.

Section 5 | Fast food geography

Unhealthy consumption geography

Direct state-level fast-food consumption was not available in the public tables used here. To avoid fake data, this map uses the real ABS state-level daily sugar-sweetened drink measure as the available unhealthy food/drink indicator.

Graph 2

Proportional symbol map: sugar-sweetened drinks

Source: ABS National Health Survey: State and territory findings, 2017-18; ABS NHS 2022 for obesity. Circle size shows daily sugar-sweetened drink consumption; colour shows obesity.
